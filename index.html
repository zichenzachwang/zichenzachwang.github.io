Zichen (Zachary) Wang

Ph.D. candidate · Schöneberg Lab, UC San Diego · San Diego, CA

I'm an imaging and computational scientist trained across wet and dry lab. My Ph.D. work spans the whole loop: designing 4D live-cell imaging experiments on lattice light-sheet microscopes, building GPU-accelerated pipelines that turn terabytes of raw data into AI-ready datasets, and developing self-supervised models that map cellular phenotypes across states. Through a deep understanding of biology paired with hands-on experimental expertise, I bring awareness of experimental caveats into data curation and model interpretation, in order to build biology-grounded AI systems with real potential for drug discovery. I finish in December 2026 and am looking to bring this approach to an industry team working on imaging-based phenomics or AI-enabled drug discovery.

Selected work

Making 4D live-cell data learnable

Living cells imaged in x, y, z and time produce data nothing off-the-shelf can handle. These four projects built the stack that can.

Cell · 2026

MitoSpace — a self-supervised map of mitochondrial phenotypes

4D spatiotemporal landscape of mitochondrial phenotypes across cellular states unlocked through representation learning

D. Agarwal*, Z. Wang*, et al.  ·  *equal contribution

A self-supervised representation-learning framework that embeds 4D lattice light-sheet volumes of mitochondria into a latent landscape where drug perturbations and cellular states organize semantically. Trained on imaging campaigns I designed and acquired, including high-content screens across 25+ drug conditions.

Paper Code Interactive atlas

Cell · 2026

ReaDDy Cell — whole-cell digital twins from 4D microscopy

Whole-cell particle-based digital twin simulations from 4D lattice light-sheet microscopy data

E. Arkfeld, Z. Wang, et al.

Particle-based whole-cell simulations built directly from 4D lattice light-sheet data on the ReaDDy reaction–diffusion engine, used to test how mitochondrial transport changes when microtubules are disrupted. My part is the imaging side: the 4D acquisitions and the GPU-accelerated processing that turns raw data into simulation-ready inputs.

Paper Code Interactive simulation

PLOS Computational Biology · 2023

MitoTNT — tracking organelle networks through time

MitoTNT: Mitochondrial Temporal Network Tracking for 4D live-cell fluorescence microscopy data

Z. Wang, et al.

Mitochondria form networks that fragment and fuse on the scale of seconds. MitoTNT follows them as graphs through 4D live-cell data, quantifying remodeling dynamics, motility and topology. Open source.

Paper Code

Journal of Microscopy · 2025

LiveLattice — real-time lattice light-sheet data, as it streams

LiveLattice: Real-time visualisation of tilted light-sheet microscopy data using a memory-efficient transformation algorithm

Z. Wang*, H. Hakozaki*, et al.  ·  *equal contribution

Lattice light-sheet microscopes write terabytes per experiment in a tilted coordinate frame nothing downstream can read. LiveLattice performs the geometric transformation with a memory-efficient algorithm fast enough to inspect data while it streams from the scope, and anchors the GPU-accelerated, OME-Zarr-based pipeline behind the lab's AI-ready datasets.
